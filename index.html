Interest-Rate Term Structures: Compatibility and Boundary Field Theory

Research on the relationship between physical interest-rate dynamics, term-structure forecasts, arbitrage-free pricing, and maturity-space equilibrium.

Sachin Oza

Recent Paper | Monograph | Contact

Research Overview

A central problem in interest-rate theory is whether a single stochastic model can simultaneously describe the evolution of observed interest rates, generate coherent forecasts of the yield curve, and support arbitrage-free pricing. These requirements are related, but they are not interchangeable: physical expectations and risk-neutral valuations are formed under different probability measures, while equilibrium restrictions impose an additional structure on the maturity dimension.

The research presented here examines those relationships through a recent paper on one-state term-structure compatibility and a monograph developing the broader instantaneous rolling forward rate (IRFR) and boundary-field approach. The objective is to identify what can be derived consistently from common assumptions, and where additional restrictions or modelling choices are unavoidable.

Recent Research Paper

A Compatibility Framework for One-State Term-Structure Models: Physical Equilibrium, Forecasts and Pricing

Author: Sachin Oza  |  Year: 2026

Overview

This paper investigates the conditions under which a one-state interest-rate model can reconcile three distinct requirements: the physical evolution of the short rate, the term structure of expected future rates, and arbitrage-free bond pricing. It distinguishes forecasts under the physical measure from those under the risk-neutral measure, rather than treating the two as interchangeable.

The analysis uses the short-rate boundary and the maturity structure of the curve to examine compatibility between observable dynamics, the market price of risk, and the restrictions implied by Heath–Jarrow–Morton pricing. It also explores how equilibrium and maturity-space consistency constrain the class of admissible one-state models, with particular attention to the distinction between necessary compatibility conditions and stronger boundary-field assumptions.

Monograph

Unified Boundary Field Theory of Interest Rates

Author: Sachin Oza

Overview

The monograph develops an instantaneous rolling forward rate representation of the term structure and examines whether curve shape, maturity-dependent volatility, equilibrium behaviour, and the influence of short-rate boundary conditions can be understood within a common mathematical framework.

Starting from the distinction between calendar-time evolution and rolling maturity, it studies the implications of maturity-invariant dynamics, coordinate closure, equilibrium stationarity, and positivity. The resulting boundary-field formulation provides a structural way of analysing the interest-rate curve, while making explicit the constraints and limits of particular model specifications.

Read alongside the recent compatibility paper, the monograph supplies the wider theoretical motivation and derivations. The paper then focuses more narrowly on the requirements for reconciling physical forecasts with risk-neutral pricing in a one-state setting.
