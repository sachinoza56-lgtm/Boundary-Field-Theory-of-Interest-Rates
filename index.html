Research into the mathematical foundations of interest-rate term structures, physical equilibrium, stochastic dynamics, forecasts and arbitrage-free pricing.

Research Overview

A fundamental question in interest-rate modelling is whether a single stochastic framework can consistently describe the observed evolution of interest rates, generate forecasts of the future term structure, and support arbitrage-free pricing.

These requirements are closely related but mathematically distinct. The physical probability measure governs realised interest-rate dynamics and expectations, while the risk-neutral measure governs the valuation of financial claims. Equilibrium introduces further restrictions on the relationship between the short rate and the maturity structure of interest rates.

This research investigates the mathematical conditions under which these properties can coexist within a one-state term-structure model.

The work is presented through a research paper establishing the compatibility framework and a thirteen-chapter monograph providing a broader mathematical development of its foundations, implications and limitations.

Research Paper

A Compatibility Framework for One-State Term-Structure Models: Physical Equilibrium, Forecasts and Pricing

Author: Sachin Oza  |  Year: 2026

Overview

This paper examines the compatibility of three fundamental requirements in one-state interest-rate models: the physical evolution of the short rate, the forecasting of future interest rates, and the arbitrage-free valuation of bonds.

The analysis distinguishes physical expectations from risk-neutral expectations and examines how their respective generators are related through the market price of risk.

By combining short-rate dynamics, the maturity structure of the yield curve and the restrictions imposed by arbitrage-free pricing, the paper investigates the conditions necessary for a coherent one-state representation.

Particular attention is given to the role of physical equilibrium, boundary consistency and the relationship between observable interest-rate dynamics and the pricing measure.

Thirteen-Chapter Research Monograph

Compatibility, Forecasts, Pricing and Physical Equilibrium of Interest Rates

Author: Sachin Oza  |  Year: 2026  |  Length: 13 Chapters

Overview

This monograph develops a systematic investigation of the relationship between physical interest-rate dynamics, equilibrium, forecasts and arbitrage-free term-structure pricing.

Its central question is whether the observed interest-rate curve, the expected evolution of future rates and the risk-neutral pricing structure can be reconciled within a common stochastic framework without introducing mutually inconsistent assumptions.

Across thirteen chapters, the analysis develops the mathematical foundations of the compatibility problem, examines the relationship between physical and risk-neutral probability measures, and explores the restrictions imposed on one-state models by equilibrium and maturity-space consistency.

The monograph examines the roles of stochastic generators, the market price of risk, boundary conditions and the relationship between the theoretical and observed term structures.

It also considers the implications of these restrictions for the admissible behaviour of interest-rate volatility and the ability of one-state models to represent the geometry of the yield curve.

The objective is not simply to introduce another parametrised interest-rate model. Rather, it is to establish which structural relationships are necessary for mathematical consistency, which additional assumptions are sufficient, and where the limitations of particular modelling approaches arise.

The monograph complements the research paper by providing a fuller discussion of the theoretical foundations, derivations and wider consequences of the compatibility framework.
